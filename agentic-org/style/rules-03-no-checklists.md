# Third pass — three rules, applied to the PROSE ONLY

The article also carries a structured evidence box ("The Record · Provenance") with
sources and fragments. That is a published feature and stays untouched. These rules
apply only to the body prose, which must not duplicate its job.

1. If a sentence explains why the preceding facts fit the article's conceit, delete it.
   The reader infers the pattern. Do not explain the resemblance, and never comment on
   your own framing ("a columnist may be supplying the resemblance").

2. Never publish a verification checklist. Lists of what evidence would settle a
   question belong in research notes. Cut sentences like "A ship name, image, terminal
   status or official statement could help clarify what happened" and "A shared cause,
   common policy or connected supply chain would support a broader explanation."

3. When expressing uncertainty, mention only the missing fact that changes the story.
   Do not enumerate every dimension the research failed to resolve.
   BAD:  "not been linked to an organism, province or daily fuel delivery"
   GOOD: "No pathogen had been identified."

Everything from the earlier passes still holds. Every fact, name, number, date and
[E] reference must survive unchanged.
