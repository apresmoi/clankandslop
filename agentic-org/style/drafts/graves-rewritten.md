Russian Ballistic Strike Halts Ukraine’s Kametstal Steel Plant

Metinvest said workers were killed at its Kamianske plant and sinter-blast production halted. The physical loss depends on how many days the furnaces stay offline.

Metinvest said a Russian ballistic strike hit its Kametstal plant in Kamianske overnight, killed workers, wounded others, damaged sinter-blast production equipment and forced a full halt at the site. [E2] Al Arabiya English, paraphrasing Reuters, reported that the strike shut a Ukrainian steel plant and killed five people. [E4]

Local reporting said two blast furnaces were offline and the fire had been extinguished. [E3] The integrated mill lost operating hours in blast-furnace and sinter production, but the tonnage loss remains unmeasured. No public restart timetable, monthly output loss or damaged-equipment inventory has been published.

Metinvest and local accounts reported five workers killed, while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy separately posted that four people were dead and five injured after the strike. [E1] Member of parliament Musa Magomedov was reported as saying five ballistic missiles hit the city, five Kametstal workers were dead, and one person was still being sought. [E3]

One blast furnace coming back would narrow the loss to a short site stoppage. A longer halt across both furnaces and sinter capacity would turn the casualty-and-damage report into a measurable production outage.

Whether the shops hit fed military production remains unconfirmed; the targeting evidence is insufficient to establish that claim. The shutdown establishes lost furnace time at Kametstal, not a disruption to strategic steel supply.

Metinvest said the strike damaged production, energy and transport equipment. [E2] A strike outside that chain would have had different consequences for the mill; this one halted furnaces and support systems alongside the damage to buildings. Those systems turn ore and coke into hot metal and allow finished steel to move.
