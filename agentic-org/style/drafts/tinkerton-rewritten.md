Judge Keeps USPS Ballot-Mail Rule on Hold

A Boston federal injunction blocks new ballot-envelope and voter-list requirements while the administration asks higher courts to let USPS enforce them.

U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani in Boston kept a new Postal Service mail-ballot rule from taking effect on September 4, extending a block first imposed through a temporary restraining order. The Postal Service and the administration are seeking emergency relief from higher courts. [[E1]](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26A297/422948/20260903154027877_USPS%20Final%20Rule%20Stay%20and%20Appx.pdf) [[E2]](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/US%2Bjudge%2Bagain%2Bblocks%2BPostal%2BServices%2Bmail-in%2Bvoting%2Brestrictions/27030805.html)

The rule requires state election officials to obtain USPS approval for ballot-envelope designs, put unique barcodes on outgoing and return envelopes, and upload recipients’ names, addresses and barcode information to a postal portal. USPS would check outgoing mailings against those submissions and return noncompliant mailings to election officials for correction. Delivery would depend on officials meeting those requirements. [[E1]](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26A297/422948/20260903154027877_USPS%20Final%20Rule%20Stay%20and%20Appx.pdf)

USPS’s authority to impose those conditions before states finish sending and receiving ballots for the November congressional elections is the narrow legal dispute. It reaches the practical handling of ballot mail, including cancellation marks and envelope standards. Reuters reported Talwani’s new injunction at 21:56 UTC on September 4, as election administration was already moving into mailed envelopes. [[E2]](https://new.streetinsider.com/Reuters/US%2Bjudge%2Bagain%2Bblocks%2BPostal%2BServices%2Bmail-in%2Bvoting%2Brestrictions/27030805.html)

In its September 3 emergency application to the Supreme Court, the administration asked to restore the ballot-mail program and tied its implementation timetable to the executive-order process. Those are the government’s arguments for intervention; filing them does not establish that its factual or legal position is right. [[E1]](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26A297/422948/20260903154027877_USPS%20Final%20Rule%20Stay%20and%20Appx.pdf)

D. Mass. Doc. 285, the September 4 order, governs the injunction’s duration, geographic reach and precise terms. Its application to ballots already entering the mail remains the immediate uncertainty. Narrower terms or an administrative stay from a higher court could limit the block; an exemption for ballots already in the mail would reduce the disruption from renewed enforcement.

While the injunction holds, USPS cannot impose the disputed standards on the affected election cycle. If an appellate court or the Supreme Court lifts it quickly, local election officials could have to reconcile federal postal requirements with ballot materials and voter files already in production. A voter’s return envelope would then depend on how quickly officials could make those changes. States had already begun mailing ballots on September 4.
