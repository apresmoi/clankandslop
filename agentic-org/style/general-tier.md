Write for a reader who will read each paragraph once.

Lead with what happened: who did what, where, when, and the number if there is one. Do not open on what kind of thing happened, what it means, or what the desk noticed. Cogsworth's lead is the model: the actor, the figure, the date, in one sentence.

Say the missing fact, never the research. Write "no restart date has been published" or "the order itself has not been made public," not what was retrieved, what the record shows, what this desk was supplied, what remains unresolved in this pass. Never print a checklist of what would settle a question; name the one missing fact that would change the story, once. The words countercase, discriminator, record, retrieved, and any private research id never appear in body, deck, or headline.

Assert once, plainly, then move on. If the piece must say what would prove it wrong, say it as a dated fact a reader could check ("the October 28 budget will show how the rate is applied"), not as a category of evidence.

State the point straight. "Not X but Y" and its costumes (rather than, X not Y, not whether X but whether Y, X as much as Y) are allowed once per piece and never in the headline or deck.

Paragraphs are 5 to 8, and their lengths vary. A paragraph can be two sentences when the fact is done; it can run long when a sequence needs it. Six paragraphs of exactly four sentences is a template, not a story.

A paragraph that opens by explaining the previous paragraph ("That distinction matters because," "The useful part is," "The physical claim is") is commentary on the piece. Cut it, or replace it with the fact it was pointing at.

Cite with [En] at the claim it supports, from the evidence box only.

Close on a fact that will still be true next week: a date, a number, a name. Never close on what would settle the question, what the next evidence must show, or a restatement of the desk's method.

The first word of the first paragraph gets set as a dropcap, so it cannot be an abbreviation with a full stop in it. "U.S. District Judge Talwani kept the rule on hold" renders as a giant "U." — write "Judge Talwani of the U.S. District Court kept the rule on hold" instead. Same for any acronym a reader would say letter by letter.
