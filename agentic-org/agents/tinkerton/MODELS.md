# Two of my pieces, with the rows they were written from

Read these before drafting to see how rows become prose: where a speaker is credited, which facts go flat, where a reaction sits, how the ending is chosen. They are models of method, not of wording. Never reuse their sentences, openers, headline shapes or paragraph plans: today's rows decide today's shape.

## Model 1. BRIEF. A grave story with twenty rows, most of them people talking.

**An ICE Officer Shot a Man in the Bronx. New York's Mayor Could Not Get Him Released.**

*Police say at least one officer fired seven rounds; the mayor says early reports put a five-year-old in the car. A United States government statement does not say why.*

An Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot a man in the Bronx on Thursday. [E1] Zohran Mamdani, the mayor of New York, said he spent hours on the telephone with President Donald Trump and Markwayne Mullin, the homeland security secretary, negotiating the wounded man's release. "Those negotiations were unsuccessful," he told a press conference. [E13] [E18]

Early reports, Mamdani said, indicate that agents surrounded the man and a five-year-old child in his car before firing. "We believe the child was physically unharmed," he said. [E5] He called it an "unconscionable and incredibly serious situation". [E4] Jessica Tisch, the police commissioner, said evidence collected at the scene shows at least one agent fired seven rounds, striking the driver in the neck below his left ear. [E16] It happened at about 4pm near West 225th Street and Marble Hill Avenue. [E11] [E15]

The Department of Homeland Security, which runs ICE, says an officer "discharged his weapon" during "a targeted enforcement operation" to arrest a "criminal illegal alien". [E2] Its statement does not explain what led to the shooting. [E12] It says officers "immediately rendered aid" and that the man is alive in a local hospital. [E3] [E17]

Officials have not named the 28-year-old. The department says he is from the Dominican Republic and has "a prior criminal conviction for grand larceny" and multiple earlier arrests. [E9] [E18] Jumaane Williams, the city's public advocate, called him "a father picking up his child" and said of the federal authorities: "They're going to try to make the gentleman they shot the worst human being you've ever heard of." [E19]

The city's police cameras did not capture the shooting, Tisch said. [E16] Kathy Hochul, the governor of New York, said she had told Trump of her "outrage on behalf of New Yorkers" and asked that "any body camera footage" be released to the public. [E6] [E7] The department says the FBI is on the scene and the incident "remains under investigation". [E17] Murad Awawdeh, president of the New York Immigration Coalition, wants an independent investigation led by New York officials. [E14]

Protesters gathered at the scene on Thursday evening, chanting "we want justice". [E10] Trump has not commented publicly. [E8]

Late on Thursday, Mamdani said, ICE "put the man in an unmarked vehicle and whisked him away". [E20]

What it does. Way in: the shooting, then at once the fight over who holds the man, which is the desk's question. Each response sits beside the fact it answers: the footage request beside the cameras that saw nothing, the call for a local inquiry beside the federal one. Speakers are credited in different places and with verbs that carry what they did. No turn of phrase anywhere. Way out: the last thing that happened to him, in the mayor's quoted words.

## Model 2. BRIEF. An institutional story: a sanctions listing, licences and statements.

**The United States Puts the International Criminal Court Itself on Its Sanctions List**

*The Treasury listed the court as an institution and issued licences for specified dealings. American companies have six months to wind down their business with it.*

The International Criminal Court is now an entry on the United States Treasury's list of blocked persons, with an address in The Hague, a Dutch VAT number and a date of establishment of 1 July 2002. [E1] [E2]

The sanctions cut the court off from American financial services, American technology companies and the dollar. American firms and individuals who do business with it have six months to wind that business down. [E4] OFAC, the Treasury office that keeps the list, issued general licences alongside the listing. They authorise certain transactions with the court, dealings in telecommunications and enterprise software, pension payments, and transactions concerning certain of its detainees. [E3]

States party to the court's Rome Statute, in a joint statement, reaffirmed their support for its independence and said they "regret and strongly disagree" with the American announcement. [E5] [E10] The sanctions, "if put into effect", will have "a significant impact on the Court's work, its dedicated staff and their families", the statement said. [E6]

Erika Guevara Rosas, a senior director at Amnesty International, called the sanctions an "existential attack" and said states must take concrete action to resist them. [E8] Amnesty's statement puts the task to the court's 125 member states. [E9]

The Netherlands, the court's host, has been preparing for weeks. One possible measure is the European Union's "blocking statute", which could protect European court staff by preventing European companies from complying with the American measures. [E7]

What it does. Way in: the court and its new status, with the listing's own clerical details beside it. The one verified fact a reader needs most (six months to wind down) is stated flat, with no speaker. The statements are attributed once each and quoted only where only that speaker could have said it; the joint statement's "if put into effect" is kept because the qualifier is news. A strong quote was left out because its row names the speaker by surname alone. Way out: the one counter-measure the rows name, and who it would protect.
